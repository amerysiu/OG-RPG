Vision

You're creating an educational word game that turns learning to read and spell into a daily ritual. It's bright, playful, and designed specifically for struggling readers using the Orton-Gillingham method. Your game meets players where they are and helps them build confidence through discovery, competition, and steady progress.

Genre and Inspirations

Your game draws from puzzle and word game masters like Wordle, Connections, Squardle, and Dordle. From Wordle you take the elegant daily challenge format and the satisfying "aha" moment of solving. From Connections and Squardle you borrow the joy of pattern recognition and wordplay. From Letterboxd you understand how community and personal connection drive engagement. Your game is casual enough to feel approachable, but educational at its core.

Look and Feel

Bright and playful is your north star. Think vibrant colors, encouraging feedback, and a mood that says "this is fun, not punishment." Every interaction feels satisfying rather than stressful. The visual language celebrates small wins and creates an atmosphere where struggling readers feel welcomed, not judged. Playful animations and satisfying sound design reinforce progress and make learning feel rewarding.

Core Mechanics

At its heart, players solve word puzzles and challenges that strengthen their reading and spelling skills through the Orton-Gillingham approach. The core loop is simple: receive a daily challenge, work through it at their own pace, discover patterns in how words work, and feel that satisfying click of mastery. Adaptive difficulty means the game meets each player's skill level. Streaks, badges, and leaderboards keep players coming back without feeling punishing. Story progression or narrative hooks create reasons to care beyond just the word puzzles themselves.

What Makes It Special

This is where education and joy collide. Most reading games feel clinical. Yours feels like something you actually want to play. The combination of daily rituals (like Wordle), adaptive difficulty that respects where learners are, and a social layer (leaderboards, real-world connection) transforms a learning tool into a game people crave. You're building something that could genuinely change how kids see reading and spelling. The fact that it's free and ad-free signals that this is about impact, not extraction.
